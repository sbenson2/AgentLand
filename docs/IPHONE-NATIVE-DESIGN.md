# Native iPhone design — 2026-10-01

The owner approved the final [interactive explorer concept](prototypes/iphone-explorer/README.md) for implementation in the real iPhone app. The phone branch uses `WorkspacePlatform.isPhone`; iPad and Mac keep their existing navigation and Home layouts.

## Navigation

New iPhone/iPad installs follow the device's appearance (System); Reset Appearance also returns
to System (owner's decision, 2026-10-06). Saved Light, Dark and System choices continue to take precedence. The
app's text follows the system's Text Size exactly (2026-10-06; it was one step larger from 2026-09-27); ⌘+, ⌘− and
⌘0 step from it and back.

One fixed header has native Liquid Glass buttons for the workspace drawer and Settings. Its centered `Workspace: task` control is a native Liquid Glass button: tapping anywhere opens the task menu, a panel that grows out of the button like the drawer and Settings (below). The workspace name is bold, the task has its kind icon, and the chevron appears when the menu has something in it. The menu is the task's title menu (HIG: the title menu holds a document's own actions): first, under the task's name, the actions the task in front offers (a chat's Model submenu (the composer's model menu, with effort), mode, Continue, Copy, Save as Markdown, Pin, Export and Delete; a terminal's Show and Hide Keyboard, Paste, Keys, Reconnect and End; an editor's Save, Reload, Copy and Find; Files' and the browser's own), with Close Task last. It holds nothing else: tasks switch only in the workspace drawer (owner's decision, 2026-10-05: "the only way to switch tabs should be in the drawer, the top button will just be the controls for that tab"); keyboard shortcuts stay. It stays this panel, as wide as the drawer, rather than a native `Menu` (owner, 2026-10-07: "Keep wide panel"), with native list rows inside (native-iOS batch 13, TM-2): the task's name is a section header in the system's own style, the buttons are the list's own row buttons with the system's highlight, in the primary colour a menu's items take, a destructive one red by its role, and a submenu row (a terminal's Keys, a chat's Model and effort, its second line in secondary text) opens its menu from a tap anywhere on the row, where a `Menu` in a list took taps on its label alone (run b12). Its buttons act once the panel has gone, as a native menu's do, so a confirmation, a sheet or the terminal's keyboard is not in the way of the panel on its way out; Hide Keyboard targets the retained terminal renderer. A key chosen from Keys closes the whole task menu, as a native submenu's choice does. The header's buttons are bar items: their text and symbols follow the text size up to Extra Extra Extra Large and stop there, and past it the Large Content Viewer shows them on a long press (the task button since batch 13; at the largest size its text had grown to fill the header). The permanent Home has no controls of its own, so it disables the control. Selection, lock/cover, workspace changes and inactive scenes dismiss it. Home is reached through the drawer's Home row.

The three header panels, the workspace drawer, Settings and the task menu, grow out of their buttons and rest just below the header (owner's instructions of 2026-10-05: grown out of their buttons like an iOS 26 menu, replacing the native popovers of 2026-10-04, then "push the menus below the header", with a compact width like Apple's menus, a quick close and no dim). A panel first takes its button's exact glass and shape over the button, then grows down to its resting frame, rounding to a menu's corners, with the content in place from the start coming into focus; it shrinks back into the same button when it closes. It rests 8 pt below the header buttons' glass (the system's standard spacing, the margin the native menu keeps from the screen's edge and the panel's own margin above the keyboard), so the three buttons stay in view and take taps while it is open: the same button closes its panel, and another closes it and opens its own. The drawer's leading edge sits on its button's leading glass edge, Settings' trailing edge on the gear's, and the task menu is centred under its button. The task menu is as wide as the drawer, the screen less the drawer button's edge inset on each side, so its rows fit on one line (owner, 2026-10-07: "the middle drop down menu needs to be wider so everything fits on one line"; it was a native `Menu`'s 250 pt, 279 at the app's default text, and its rows wrapped), and as tall as its rows up to 70% of the screen. The drawer and Settings are the screen's width less their button's edge inset on each side and take all the height below the header, down to the bottom margin or the keyboard (owner's instruction, 2026-10-05: "drawer needs to be wider and like settings take up the whole screen as much as it can under the header"). Every panel stays on screen and above the keyboard. Geometry and motion were measured against a native SwiftUI `Menu` on the same glass buttons on the same simulator (`-AgentLandPhoneReferenceMenu`): 250 pt wide, its corners fit a 24 pt circle, it grows with a quick spring that overshoots about 8% and settles, and it goes in well under 0.1 s; the panels use a 24 pt continuous corner, a spring at the app's one speed to open (0.25 s, `AgentLandMotion.drawer`) and a 0.1 s close (`AgentLandMotion.drawerClose`). Nothing is dimmed. A tap anywhere outside the panel and the header's buttons closes it and reaches nothing behind it (the region is an element of its own, "Dismiss", as UIKit's is). VoiceOver treats the panel as modal, as it does an iOS menu: the header and the content are hidden from it while a panel is open, and its escape closes it; Escape on a hardware keyboard closes it, and Reduce Motion fades the whole panel in place. One header panel shows at a time. The drawer and Settings each hold a navigation stack; their pages push inside the same panel with a Back button.

The drawer is the one place tasks switch. It has three sections and nothing else (owner's request, 2026-10-06: "three tabs (the large pill with three options that slides the highlight to the current one) … nothing else in the left drawer"): a native segmented control at its top, at the large control size with a little more room around it than the tabs before it, chooses Workspace, Files or Git, and stays there over a pushed page. The last section used stays while the app runs (Workspace at first); choosing another goes back to its root. **Workspace** is one native inset grouped List (paper or slate cells and separators on the panel's own raised platter, 2026-10-07), every workspace as a tree: a row per workspace (its name, folder, Git badge, activity and use, as the Workspaces screen shows it, but the one in use is highlighted rather than checked, and at the accessibility text sizes the words stack in the row's whole width without the folder symbol, the folder on one line truncated in the middle), then Chats (the standalone plain chats) when there are any or the host offers them, then any unavailable workspace that still has tasks, marked Unavailable. Each group has its own chevron button (at least 44 pt) that opens it to every open task in it (chats, terminals, editors, browsers), each with its kind's symbol as the header and the task menu show it (DR-4, 2026-10-07), the task in front on a lighter highlight of its own (owner's request, 2026-10-06: "highlight it and also sub highlight the active tab"); the group in use starts open, and a group opened or closed by hand stays so while the app runs. A workspace row's tap makes it the workspace in use and shows its Home, and the drawer closes; on Chats or an unavailable workspace the tap opens or closes the group. A task's tap shows it and closes the drawer (the task in front is checked; a swipe reveals Close and a long press Reload and Close Task). A workspace row carries the Workspaces screen's actions with the same confirmations: swipe trailing to Remove or Close, leading to Rename, the same items in its context menu and VoiceOver's actions; Adding a workspace is Home's (its Workspace button's Choose Folder), as is Resume, which lists the workspaces to pick one up again (owner, 2026-10-06: "resume lives on the home screen… same thing with adding a workspace, just on home, not in the drawer"). **Files** is the workspace in use's whole Files surface, the page titled with the folder it shows (the workspace's name at its top) and no folder bar: the title menu lists the folders above it, the bar's ⋯ menu holds New File (an alert, as New Folder) and New Folder, uploads from Files and Photos, Show Hidden Files (a toggle), Sort By (a picker, with an Ascending toggle) and Refresh, and the name and content search is the navigation bar's search field, under the title (at the bottom it floated over the rows and, at the largest text sizes, took no tap) (native-iOS batch 8, FI-1, FI-6, FI-15). Rows are the Files app's list rows: a folder's leading disclosure turns down and opens it in place and its row opens it; a file's row is a navigation link with the system's chevron, its size the trailing value; rename, move, copy path, share and Trash or delete with their confirmations are the row's swipes, context menu and VoiceOver actions (no ⋯ button, FI-2, FI-3). Uploads are a bar under the list with the system's progress views (FI-7). A file's row pushes its read-only inspector (the text the editor would load, monospaced, scrollable and selectable; a file over 256 KB or not text says Can't Preview This File), whose Open in Workspace opens its editor task and closes the drawer. The editor's band names the file's path and says Edited and the caret's line in secondary text beside a glass Save; Copy and Reload are the task menu's; a conflict shows its reason in place with bordered choices, the two that replace a side destructive and still confirmed (FI-9, FI-10). **Git** is the native tree below, its History open from the start. Files and Git show "Choose a Workspace" while none is in use. Home, Resume Work, More Workspaces, Hide and Workspaces… are not in the drawer any more: Resume Work opens as a sheet from where it is offered (on iPhone that was an empty agent chat's Resume Work tile until every chat took the plain chat's landing on 2026-10-07; since then it is Home's Resume box, below, which also opens the Workspaces screen), and a workspace's management is its row's. Git/Files never become working-task rows; opened documents do. Selection, close confirmations, retained sessions and draft recovery remain under WorkspaceModel.

Settings is the gear's panel: the app's Settings list with each category pushed inside it; its sheets and confirmations (Pair Another Host, Memory's editor) present over it. Its list and pages are native grouped forms on the panel's raised paper or slate with paper or slate rows (2026-10-06; tone 2026-10-07): the host's status as the first row, as the account is in the Settings app; sections with the system's header and footer text; pages opened by rows with a symbol and the system's chevron; values as `LabeledContent`; Disconnect and Clear Cached Chats as plain buttons in the accent, as the Settings app draws actions that lose nothing for good (Clear's confirmation carries the destructive role), Pair Another Host in a section of its own. The controls inside the pages are the system's (2026-10-06, batch 4): switches with their explanation as the row's subtitle, Appearance's System, Light and Dark as a checkmarked list, the saved hosts and the default model as rows with the accent's checkmark, Terminal's size Stepper and cursor Picker, row menus as `ellipsis.circle` in the accent (saved hosts also swipe to Forget), the Agents page's search field in its navigation bar, `ProgressView` while loading and `ContentUnavailableView` when empty. A state shows in place as a section of its own (the host isn't connected, a read failed, with Try Again); a change that failed is the system's alert; a plain success shows only in its row.

The keyboard goes before any header panel opens and does not come back when it closes. Anything that opens Files or Git on iPhone (Browse Files, Git, a folder from Resume Work's search) opens the drawer on that section and leaves the task in front. Resume Work's search sits at the top, above the keyboard, in its sheet. In landscape with the keyboard up the header makes way for the surface, as Notes and Mail do, and comes back with the keyboard gone. Notices follow the native pattern (owner's decision, 2026-10-06): a refusal or failure in the drawer's Git or Files section, the Workspaces screen, pairing or a new task is the system's alert, in sight wherever the person scrolled to act; a plain success shows only in the list (and is announced to VoiceOver); a folder that could not be read says so in place, with Try Again; the one-time notifications tip is TipKit's.

## Home

The four actions are Chat, Terminal, Workspace and Resume (owner's request, 2026-10-04): larger one-line Liquid Glass buttons in a 2×2 grid, symbol and title centred in each button. A tap grows the button's own Liquid Glass upward into one box, flowing into the other buttons on the way and shrinking back into the same button on close, with the capability-driven providers, programs or workspaces (each agent row shows that agent's official registry icon through the host); the choices are a native plain list (row buttons with the system's highlight, clear over the glass, the separator token between them; native-iOS batch 11, HO-2), and the box is as tall as they need, up to the top of Home's visible area wherever Home has scrolled, and the list scrolls beyond that, so there is no pagination (B-22: at the largest text sizes, with Home scrolled to Chat, the box used to open below the screen; Home now holds still while a box is open). Workspace's box browses the Mac's folders from Developer, with the path to go back, Already a workspace and Git marks, New Folder (New Workspace…) and Open, which adds a folder as a workspace first when it is not one. Resume's box (owner's choice, 2026-10-07) starts with what iPad's Resume Work menu holds: Search History, which opens the Resume Work sheet with its search field focused, then Archived Chats and Terminal Logs when the host keeps them; then the workspaces to pick one up again; then Workspaces…, the Workspaces screen, when the host manages its workspaces. Search History keeps Resume available with no workspaces. A choice closes the box at once and its sheet comes up over Home. Home's art dims behind the box; ×, Escape, a tap outside, lock and covers close it. The native menus of 2026-10-01 were replaced. Ordinary Home remains bounded; accessibility sizes stack the actions in one column and may scroll the launcher. In landscape the tent sits beside the actions and the action area drops the clear band it keeps above its buttons in portrait (the quip already has its own gap there), so the buttons keep their size and 32 pt above the bottom; portrait is unchanged (2026-10-05).

## Controls

The shared iOS control and primary styles delegate to SwiftUI’s `.glass` and `.glassProminent` renderers. Standalone controls and Home/landing actions use those styles. In chat (native-iOS batch 6, 2026-10-07) Send and Stop are `.glassProminent` circles, Jump to Latest a `.glass` circle and the docked needs-you pill a `.glass` capsule, the two in one `GlassEffectContainer`, and the composer the system's glass alone (no hairline, no hand-made Reduce Transparency fallback) in a bottom `safeAreaBar`; approval and question cards are content, so their buttons are `.bordered` capsules with Allow Once and Send Answers `.borderedProminent`; the chat's icon and text buttons (Copy, Run Again, Details, the attach menu, Queue, dictation, queue edit and remove, Show More, Something Else…) are `.borderless`, and its cards, queue rows, chips and the rewind banner are their paper or slate fill without an outline (a border only under Increase Contrast). In batch 7 (2026-10-07) the transcript's disclosures (reasoning, tool rows and groups, a continued chat's context) became the system's `DisclosureGroup`, with its own chevron in the secondary text colour and its own expanded or collapsed state for VoiceOver; a question's options the system's button toggles (`.toggleStyle(.button)`, the chosen one in the selected tint); and the composer's mode menu an inline picker that checks the mode in use. Embedded composer options use native `.bordered` controls. Native toolbar buttons, plain content rows, standard toggles and pickers retain their system behavior. The terminal keyboard accessory keeps its sideways-scrolling keys (owner's decision, no `UIToolbar`) in UIKit’s `.glass()` configuration without manually drawn keycaps or shadows; since batch 12 (2026-10-07) their labels and symbols use the body text style (the lettered keys in its monospaced design) in the label colour and follow the text size up to Extra Extra Extra Large (the bar's `maximumContentSizeCategory`), past which the Large Content Viewer shows them, and Ctrl is a toggle button (`changesSelectionAsPrimaryAction`, so VoiceOver hears it selected) that latches into `.prominentGlass()` in the window's tint, the app's accent, with UIKit's own label colour on it. Mac button rendering remains as before. Dynamic Type text roles remain.

## Colours

Since 2026-10-07 the iPhone and iPad interface is the owner's paper and slate everywhere (owner's choice "B: Paper & slate", after the colour audit in `.evidence/tests-repair-20261005/review-20261006/colour-audit/`; it replaces the system backgrounds of 2026-10-06 and reverses the native pass's Q1 and Q11). The covers keep the brand slate. The Mac keeps the canvas behind its lists and the system's rows in its forms. Alerts, context menus and the keyboard stay the system's, which cannot take the tone; Liquid Glass buttons read the tone behind them; the segmented control's track stays the system's.

## Git

The native tree has nested staged, unstaged and untracked changes, diffs, stage/unstage and commit, local/remote branches, paged history and commit files, configured remotes, and saved stashes. Since native-iOS batch 9 (2026-10-07) it is a sidebar list on the drawer panel's platter, as Mail lists mailboxes: the repository, branch and upstream as values (warnings in the section's footer with the warning symbol), then sections that fold from their headers, each with its count: Conflicts, Staged (Unstage All in its header), Unstaged and Untracked (Stage All in each header), shown only with files, then Branches, Remote Branches, History (open from the start, earlier commits loading as the list reaches its end), Remotes and Stashes. A file shows its status as a coloured symbol (VoiceOver reads it in words); a tap shows its diff, and its trailing swipe, context menu or VoiceOver action stages or unstages it, as on iPad. A branch row switches to it, with a checkmark on the current one; its swipe or menu deletes it after a confirmation. New Branch… asks for the name in an alert, Stash Changes… is a sheet holding the message and the untracked-files switch (Save is the confirmation), a remote's Fetch, Pull and Push are in its menu, and a stash applies or drops by its swipe or menu. A commit or a stash pushes its page in the drawer: which commit, who and when, and its changed files, each opening its diff. The commit bar is a band across the page's foot, above the keyboard: the message, what is staged and who the commit is made as, and the prominent glass Commit (⌘↩); at an accessibility text size only the message and Commit, so the list keeps its room, with who commits a Repository row (Commits As). Pull to refresh or the bar's Refresh reads it again, also while a read runs (the newer read replaces it). Diff sheets close with the system's close button and sit on the sheet's own tone. The host advertises each feature; older hosts keep the operations they support. Extended controls require the updated host implementation. See [the host contracts](HOST-API.md).

Host mutations stay authenticated and scoped to configured projects/repositories, retain locking and deadlines, and never retry an unknown outcome. Pull is fast-forward only. Force operations, merge/rebase and conflict editing remain on the Mac. Remote credential, askpass, hook and filter programs are not executed by these routes. Destructive branch/stash/discard actions ask for confirmation.
